# You Are What You Eat

*By Tim Dort-Golts, Bordeaux Chapter*

In applied AI, the single biggest determinant of output quality is not the model. It is the context.

The quality, adequateness, and richness of what you feed alongside your instructions is what separates a generic-sounding email from precise communication in your voice. It is what separates a strategy document that misses the mark from one informed by every relevant parameter. More high-signal context, better output. This is not controversial. Anyone who has spent real time building with these tools knows it immediately.

The thing is, this is no less true for humans.

Your ability to perform well in any situation is directly tied to the context you carry into it. General knowledge. Similar situations you have been in before. How much you know about the people, the problem, the stakes. Give the same person two different briefings and you get two radically different performances. The variable is not talent. It is context.

Think about the difference between walking into a meeting cold and walking into one where you have read every document, talked to every stakeholder, and thought through every angle. Same brain. Same skills. Completely different output.

Now go one level deeper. Context is not just information. Energy is context too. And energy is shaped by environment.

The content you consume. The music you listen to. The people you talk to every day. The problems you choose to spend your time on. The broader culture you operate in. All of it feeds into the state you bring to your work. Surround yourself with high-agency people and your baseline rises. Sit long enough in a low-energy environment and it quietly pulls yours down.

Here is the uncomfortable part: you do not notice the drift in real time. A language model does not know it is being fine-tuned on bad data. It just starts producing worse outputs and has no idea why. You work the same way. Spend enough time in a culture where the default is slow, cautious, and permission-seeking, and you start to believe that is normal. Your outputs degrade. Not because you lost ability, but because your context changed.

"You are the average of the five people you spend the most time with." "You are what you eat." People treat these like motivational posters. They are not. They are descriptions of how context works, and the applied AI age proves the mechanism. We now have a technical framework that demonstrates, measurably, that the same engine with different context produces wildly different results. The engine does not change. The context does.

You are the engine. Your environment, your relationships, your daily inputs: that is your context window.

Curate it deliberately. Seek out people with taste, energy, and high standards. Feed your mind with material that raises the bar. Put yourself in rooms where the pace is fast and mediocrity is not tolerated. The old wisdom was always right. Now we can see exactly why.
