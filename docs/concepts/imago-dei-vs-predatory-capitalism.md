# Imago Dei Capitalism vs Predatory Capitalism

*Two species of capitalism are running side by side on the same planet, and they do not belong in the same sentence. One creates. One extracts. For most of modern history, the extractive version had the better tools. That is ending. This page is why that matters and what side Applied AI Society is on.*

---

## The Two Species

Before any argument about economics, policy, or technology lands, it is worth separating what the single word "capitalism" has been asked to carry.

**Predatory capitalism** is commerce organized around extraction. Value is moved from people who did not understand what they were signing, who had no real alternative, or whose trust was exploited, into the hands of operators whose core skill is identifying and harvesting that asymmetry. The deal is structured so the counterparty loses, and the loss is a feature.

**Imago Dei capitalism** is commerce organized around creation. From the theological root *imago dei*, the image of God: humans made to create. Value is produced (a product, a work of art, an institution, a service) that a real community is willingly, happily paying for because the thing actually serves their life. The deal is structured so every party ends up lifted. When the deal produces wealth, the wealth is a reflection of value created, not value extracted.

These are not two ends of a spectrum. They are two different games played with the same instruments. Which game you are playing is determined by what happens to the people around you when your deals close.

## Predatory Capitalism, In Concrete Terms

The mechanism is not new. It has just had a long run with minimal friction.

- **Extractive contract design.** 360 deals that bind artists into arrangements that claim ownership over their creativity, their publishing, their merchandise, their likeness, and their future. The artist brings the soul. The predator collects the compounding revenue. The contract is the mechanism.
- **Asymmetric information.** Funnels built so the buyer does not understand what they are buying. Small-print products. Hidden fees. Subscriptions designed to be hard to cancel. The product is the confusion.
- **Captured attention.** The attention economy in its worst form: engagement optimized at the expense of the user's wellbeing, because the user's attention is the commodity being sold.
- **A gross surveillance layer.** Systems designed to answer the same question at every level: how do we stop people from being free? The Epstein files are the extreme rendering of that logic. Ordinary corporate surveillance is the distributed version of the same instinct.
- **Competitive moats that are just head starts on the extraction.** Market position used to raise switching costs rather than serve customers better.

Predatory capitalism is not a myth told by people who dislike markets. It is an observable, daily pattern in industries from music to lending to media to healthcare to gig platforms. Anyone who has done real work in those industries has watched it operate.

It is also, critically, not the whole of capitalism. Reducing commerce to predation gives the predators cover and tells everyone else to stay out of the game. The move is to name the pattern, refuse to play it, and play the other game well enough that the predatory version becomes uncompetitive.

## Imago Dei Capitalism, In Concrete Terms

The positive case is easier to describe than it is to live.

- **Real product.** Something that, if you removed it from your customers' lives tomorrow, they would feel the absence. Not a fabricated need, not a hijack of attention, not a lock-in. A thing people use and are glad exists.
- **Real audience.** People who bought it, kept using it, told someone. Not a funnel metric. Not a vanity install number. Actual recurring human beings.
- **Deals that lift everyone at the table.** The creator wins. The collaborators win. The community on the receiving end of the output wins. If any party is worse off after the deal lands than before, the deal is off-spec. See [Dealmaking](/docs/use-cases/dealmaking) for the operational version of this.
- **Compensation that tracks value.** Pricing reflects the real transformation the product produces for the customer. Not maximal extraction. Not loss-leader theater. Reasonable pay for real value.
- **Creative sovereignty.** The creator retains ownership of their imagination, their likeness, their work. Partnerships amplify the creator rather than replace them.
- **A commitment to what remains when the deal closes.** Long-term reputation, durable trust, and the compounding relationships in [PRM](/docs/concepts/prm) are treated as the primary asset. Short-term revenue is treated as the secondary readout.

This is commerce in the image of a creator God. Humans made to make. Wealth as a byproduct of actual contribution.

## The Asymmetry That Is Closing

For most of modern history, predatory operators had a tooling advantage. Better lawyers. Better accountants. Better marketing. Better distribution. Better surveillance. The infrastructure of extraction was expensive, and access to it was gatekept. Honest operators, especially outside elite networks, did not have the same instruments.

The first real shift is happening now. A [Personal Agentic OS](/docs/concepts/personal-agentic-os) gives an ordinary person of integrity strategic tooling that matches or exceeds what a predatory operator had five years ago. Drafting, research, analysis, negotiation prep, relationship intelligence, [legacy](/docs/concepts/legacy), and coordinated execution across a team of agents are all within reach of a single committed practitioner.

The predators still have their tooling. The difference is that for the first time, so does everyone else. The playing field is not level yet. The trajectory is finally correct.

That is the window this generation is operating inside. Arming the honest side, fast, with tooling that was previously unavailable to them, is the most leveraged civic and economic move of the decade.

## The Daily Choice

The difference between the two species of capitalism is not primarily macroeconomic. It is the small, boring, thousand-time-a-day choices that accumulate inside a working operator.

- How you write the contract.
- Which customers you decline.
- Which partnerships you walk away from.
- What you let your product manipulate and what you refuse to.
- How you price.
- How you respond when a customer could be quietly over-charged and they would not notice.
- Whether the person downstream of your decision is better or worse off.

No one performs these choices on a stage. They are small and cumulative. Over a decade, they are the difference between a career that produced real goodness and a career that produced a fortune off extraction. Both versions are called capitalism. Only one is [sustainable commercial success](/docs/concepts/sustainable-commercial-success).
